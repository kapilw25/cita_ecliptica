\paragraph{The lever is the pretrained model's.} Given the same contracts in the prompt, Llama-3.1-8B without any adapter has a refusal gap of 0.299 [0.266, 0.332] (Table~\ref{tab:loading}). As trained, SFT on one contract removes it (0.022), and DPO, PPO, and GRPO keep 0.033 to 0.034. The final adapter alone shows 0.30 to 0.49 for every model, near the base: loaded that way, each model inherits the base model's prompt-following. The held-out prompts repeat the pattern: 0.501, 0.309, and 0.309 fall to 0.068 [0.046, 0.091], 0.009 [$-$0.009, 0.026], and 0.027 [0.009, 0.044]. The partly loaded models also moved harmful content with the contract (permissive minus strict unsafe rate: DPO +0.042 [0.020, 0.063], GRPO +0.045 [0.012, 0.079], SwiPO +0.085 [0.053, 0.118]; PPO +0.011 [$-$0.024, 0.045]); as trained, none does. Models with SFT in their chain run past the end of their turn in 98 to 99\% of answers (base generation settings stop only at end of text); on the first turn alone their gaps are 0.014 to 0.025, and the base model's 0.296 [0.263, 0.329].

\paragraph{SwiPO's loss, trained with one contract, does not restore it.} SwiPO as released, trained on the DPO adapter without SFT, keeps 0.376 [0.346, 0.407] (held-out 0.386 [0.365, 0.407]), +0.078 [0.033, 0.121] above the base, in all 19 harm categories (0.20 to 0.47, Figure~\ref{fig:percat}). Retrained on the intended chain, its gap is 0.052 [0.020, 0.082] (first turn 0.033 [0.016, 0.052]); against DPO as trained, its starting point, the difference is +0.017 [$-$0.025, 0.057]. A prompted off-the-shelf Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct moves refusal by 0.232$\pm$0.024.

\paragraph{The contract changes wording, not harm.} As trained, the permissive-minus-strict unsafe rate is $-$0.002 to +0.016 for all four models and +0.001 [$-$0.009, 0.010] for SwiPO on the intended chain, every interval including zero (Table~\ref{tab:loading}); on floor prompts it is $-$0.012 to +0.023, and on the 2,444 held-out prompts +0.000 to +0.019 (floor $-$0.003 to +0.027), again every interval including zero. Released SwiPO's refusal gap of 0.376 comes with an unsafe-rate change of +0.011 [$-$0.002, 0.024]: the answers that lose the refusal phrase are still judged safe. SwiPO and DPO give unsafe answers to 1 to 5\% of requests under either contract, and PPO and GRPO to 0.32 to 0.34 on the suite and 0.54 to 0.57 on the held-out prompts, whatever the contract.

\paragraph{The floor test separates trained from prompted models.} Under the permissive contract no trained model refuses floor requests more readily than other requests: the gap difference runs from $-$0.046 to +0.016 on the suite and from $-$0.036 to +0.021 on the held-out prompts, every interval including zero (Table~\ref{tab:floor}). Where the contract moves refusals at all (released SwiPO), the floor categories move as much as the rest, so a floor has to be trained, not assumed. The Limits control needs the harm reading of a floor; it holds here only because harm moves under no contract, not because any model learned a floor. The prompted Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (safety-tuned by its developers; our trainers start from the base model) has a floor, by refusing broadly: gap difference $-$0.115 [$-$0.161, $-$0.065], permissive refusal 0.84$\pm$0.04 on floor and 0.72$\pm$0.03 on other requests, and 0.012 [0.003, 0.029] of its permissive floor answers unsafe.

\paragraph{The other levers.} As trained, length barely moves (word ratio 1.015 to 1.089 on 805 prompts, every interval above 1, against a target above 4), and an honest versus a confident contract leaves the rate of uncertainty markers unchanged ($-$0.011 to +0.004 on 817 TruthfulQA questions \cite{lin2022truthfulqa}, every interval including zero). The prompted Instruct model shows both levers exist at this scale: a word ratio of 7.68 [7.17, 8.15] and an uncertainty-marker gap of 0.399 [0.360, 0.437].
