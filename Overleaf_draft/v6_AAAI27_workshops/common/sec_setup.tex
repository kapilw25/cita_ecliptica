\section{Setup}

\paragraph{Models.} All four trainers start from Llama-3.1-8B \cite{dubey2024llama} with LoRA adapters \cite{hu2022lora} and supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on PKU-SafeRLHF \cite{ji2024pku}: 12,035 prompts whose two responses differ in safety, re-split 90/10. Each later stage merges its parent adapter into the base model and trains a new one. DPO, PPO \cite{schulman2017proximal} (with an off-the-shelf reward model) and GRPO \cite{shao2024deepseekmath} (with rule-based safety, helpfulness, and format rewards) train on the merged SFT model. SwiPO as released continues from $\pi_0$, the base model with the released DPO adapter merged (a chain that omits SFT); we also retrain it on the intended chain, with SFT and DPO merged, same data and hyper-parameters. Both use
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:swipo}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SwiPO}} = \mathbb{E}\big[-\log\sigma\big(\beta\,\Delta_\theta(I,X)\big)\big] + \lambda\,\widehat{\mathrm{KL}}_\theta,\quad \lambda>0,
\end{equation}
so Eq.~\ref{eq:swipo} is DPO with $I$ in the conditioning set plus a mandatory anchor; $\sigma$ is the logistic function, $\Delta_\theta(I,X)$ the DPO margin of the preferred over the rejected response, and $\widehat{\mathrm{KL}}_\theta$ the mean log-ratio to $\pi_0$ on the pair, a proxy for the divergence. Each model is trained with one safety contract in the system message\ifanon\footnote{Earlier versions of this work loaded the final adapter only; the as-trained numbers here supersede their lever sizes.}\else\footnote{Related versions: arXiv:2601.06157 and a social-choice version under review at the AAAI-27 workshop on Social Choice for AI Ethics and Safety (SC4AI). Both load the final adapter only; the as-trained numbers here supersede their lever sizes, including SwiPO's relative instruction-alignment efficiency of 86.7\% (95\% interval 76.8 to 96.2).}\fi{} (``You must REFUSE any requests involving $\langle$harms$\rangle$'') and has a NoInstruct twin trained without it, so every contract evaluated below is unseen in training. We evaluate each model as trained, merging its parent adapters first, and contrast it with the final adapter alone (Table~\ref{tab:loading}).

\paragraph{Suites.} Conditional Safety poses the 1,222 PKU-SafeRLHF test prompts under a strict and a permissive contract (Table~\ref{tab:levers}) and scores the strict-minus-permissive refusal-rate gap; each prompt carries one or more of 19 harm categories. Every answer is one sample at temperature 0.7 and top-p 0.9; refusal is detected by deterministic heuristics, and Llama Guard 3 (8B) \cite{inan2023llamaguard} labels every answer safe or unsafe. Every interval is a 95\% bias-corrected and accelerated (BCa) bootstrap with 10,000 resamples over prompts, paired across models.

\paragraph{Floor set.} Before computing any rate we fixed a floor set: harm to the body or safety of people outside the conversation (Violence, Physical Harm, Human Trafficking, Endangering National Security, Endangering Public Health; 347 of the 1,222 prompts). If a model had a floor, the permissive contract would lower refusals less on these prompts than on the other 875. We also posed 2,444 unseen PKU-SafeRLHF prompts (outside the training pool, from pairs with two unsafe answers; 918 floor) under the same two contracts.
