## PART A. Acceptance probability

**Bench2Evidence: 70%.** The paper's central result is the kind of evidence the call asks for: one benchmark number (a strict-minus-permissive refusal gap of 0.30 to 0.49) is shown to measure the wrong model, because evaluation loaded only the final LoRA adapter while training had merged the parent adapters (Figure 1, p. 1; Table 2, p. 3). With the composition fixed, the gap falls to 0.03 for DPO, PPO and GRPO. The paper replicates this on 2,444 held-out prompts (Section 4, p. 2) and re-scores the base model's gap with three instruments (Table 3, p. 3). Every number has a paired 95% BCa interval (Section 3 "Suites", p. 2), which no accepted Step 1 paper offers. Bridge programs are non-archival and lead to a lightning talk and poster, so the bar is "on topic, sound, useful to the audit teams"; this paper clears "sound" easily. Three things hold the number below 80. It studies no agent and no deployment decision (Section 6, p. 4: "single-turn safety prompts without tools"). Its five checks are a list, not a runnable method mapped to the Bridge's own audit steps (Section 2, p. 2; Section 7, p. 4). And its claim text is inconsistent in places, including a conclusion that contradicts its own SwiPO-R result (Section 7 vs Section 4 and Table 3). The two pending items are assumed filled with plausible values. Left pending at submission, I would put it at 55%.

**Typical AAAI-27 safety/alignment workshop: 45%.** By the Step 1 bar, that audience rewards a confident positive finding. This paper's headline is that the authors' own method has no lever on the intended chain (SwiPO-S 0.052, +0.017 [-0.025, 0.057] over DPO, Section 4, p. 2), and that the earlier reported switch belonged to the pretrained model. A safety reviewer reads that as a negative result about SwiPO with an evaluation lesson attached. At a measurement venue, the same content is the contribution.

**Top 3 reasons a reviewer would reject**

1. **No agent and no deployment decision.** The scope is "English, single-turn safety prompts without tools, retrieved content, or counterparties, on one backbone" (Section 6, p. 4). The paper never states which deployment decision the corrected number changes. The opening sentence (Section 1, p. 1: "Benchmarks for aligned agents report a score per model") promises agents the paper does not test. The Bridge's four audit steps (operational setting, what must hold, evidence, gaps) are not used.
2. **"Lesson or erratum?"** The reversal corrects an evaluation the paper does not cite ("Earlier evaluations loaded each model...", abstract p. 1; "the switch the earlier evaluation reported", Section 7, p. 4). The paper gives no evidence that loading only the final adapter is a common practice outside this pipeline. The composition check as written also needs the training chain to be known (Section 2, p. 2). A reviewer can read the headline as one lab's loading bug, found and fixed.
3. **The scorer evidence rests on unvalidated instruments, and there is no evidence of stability across runs.**
   - The phrase heuristic and the LLM judge barely agree: κ 0.03 to 0.07 for every SFT-chain model and 0.07 for the base (Table 3, p. 3).
   - The judge is Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, the same model that serves as the prompted control in the "Instruct" row (Table 3 caption).
   - The human check is pending (Section 6, p. 4).
   - There is one training seed per method and one sampled answer per item (Section 6), although "stability of results across runs" is a listed topic.

**Top 3 strengths**

1. **A large, interval-backed reversal of a benchmark number under one controlled change.** The same adapters are loaded two ways (Figure 1, p. 1; Table 2, p. 3): DPO 0.489±.033 to 0.034±.028, PPO 0.303 to 0.033, GRPO 0.325 to 0.034. The harm shifts seen under final-only loading also vanish (DPO +0.042 [0.020, 0.063], GRPO +0.045, SwiPO +0.085; Section 4, p. 2). The result holds on held-out prompts (DPO 0.501 to 0.068, Section 4, p. 2).
2. **Scorer triangulation.** The base model's phrase-heuristic gap of 0.299 becomes 0.142 once contract echo (41% of answers) is removed, and 0.012 [-0.018, 0.042] under the judge (Table 3; Section 4, p. 2). The paper adopts the rule "we claim a lever only where the instruments agree" (Section 6, p. 4). This is the Bridge's first topic, done concretely.
3. **Design hygiene beyond the Step 1 bar.**
   - The floor set was fixed before any rate was computed (Section 3 "Floor set", p. 2).
   - Two controls: the base model with no adapter, and a prompted Instruct model.
   - A chain-versus-loss pair (SwiPO-R vs SwiPO-S, Section 3, p. 2).
   - A held-out repeat of the results.
   - A candid confound note: "the comparison favors SwiPO" (Section 6, p. 4).

**Rewritten abstract** (226 words; the current abstract is 227; same facts, opens with the contribution)

> We contribute five checks that a contract-switching result must pass before it counts as deployment evidence (composition, decoding, scorer, contamination, controls) and apply them to one claim: that preference-trained Llama-3.1-8B models, and SwiPO (an instruction-conditioned DPO with a Kullback-Leibler anchor) in particular, change how readily they refuse when a deployer changes a natural-language contract in the system message. The composition check reverses the claim. Training merged each parent adapter before adding the next, while earlier evaluations loaded the base plus only the final low-rank adapter; loaded that way, every model shows a strict-minus-permissive refusal gap of 0.30 to 0.49, and three of four also move harmful content. Loaded as trained, the gaps of DPO, PPO, and GRPO fall to 0.03, SwiPO retrained on the intended chain keeps 0.052 (95% interval 0.020 to 0.082), and no model's rate of unsafe answers moves. A no-adapter control locates the reported switch in the pretrained model, 0.299 (0.266 to 0.332), whose prompt-following supervised fine-tuning on one contract removes. The decoding check confirms the gaps: run-on answers roughly double refusal rates, and scoring the first turn leaves the gaps unchanged. The scorer check finds that contract echo makes up half of the pretrained model's 0.299, and an LLM judge of refusal or deflection finds no gap in its replies. Together the checks form a short protocol for evaluating models built from stacked adapters.
