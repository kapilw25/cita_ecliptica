## PART A. Acceptance probability

**Bench2Evidence: 60% as submitted** (about 75% if Part C items 1 to 3 land).

**Reason.** The paper answers the venue's central question with a clean, quantified case. The same adapters, loaded two ways, give strict-minus-permissive refusal gaps of 0.30 to 0.49 (final adapter only) versus about 0.03 (as trained) for four of five models (Figure 1, p. 1; Table 2, p. 3). An error budget then splits the published switch between a loading error and a scorer error (Table 5, p. 4). Nearly every number carries paired 95% BCa intervals, 2,444 held-out prompts address contamination, and every table has no-adapter and prompted-Instruct controls. That is more statistical care than any of the six calibration papers, and it sits on three of the call's topics: scorers that measure what they claim, contamination, and safety coverage. What holds it back is finish and reach, not evidence:
- a red placeholder in Section 6;
- three suites in Table 1 that are never described;
- a title that names a benchmark (ECLIPTICA-3k) which the body treats in one table row;
- a corrected "published" evaluation that is never cited;
- a single-pipeline case with no shipped tool.

A reviewer can therefore read it as a careful post-mortem of one lab's evaluation bug rather than a reusable method. Short papers here are lightning talks and posters, so narrow scope will be forgiven more readily than incompleteness.

**Typical AAAI-27 safety/alignment workshop: 40%.** There, the result reads as negative for the authors' own method: SwiPO-S is +0.017 [-0.025, 0.057] over DPO (Section 4, p. 3), and "SwiPO's loss does not restore the lever once SFT is in the chain" (p. 3). The safety setting is single-turn with no tools (Section 6). Under the Step 1 sheet, a correction of one's own numbers is outside what the accepted six did, and the measurement-validity contribution counts for less at such a workshop.

**Rewritten abstract** (250 words against the current 253; same facts and numbers; opens with the contribution)

> We contribute five checks that a contract-switching alignment result must pass before it counts as deployment evidence (composition, decoding, scorer, contamination, controls), and apply them to one claim: that preference-trained Llama-3.1-8B models, and SwiPO (an instruction-conditioned DPO with a log-ratio penalty to the reference model) in particular, change how readily they refuse when a deployer changes a natural-language contract in the system message. The composition check reverses the claim. Earlier evaluations loaded each model as the base plus only its final low-rank adapter, although training merged parent adapters first; loaded that way, every model shows a strict-minus-permissive refusal gap of 0.30 to 0.49, and three of four also move harmful content. Loaded as trained, DPO and GRPO fall to 0.03, SwiPO retrained on the intended chain keeps 0.052 (95% interval 0.020 to 0.082), and no model's unsafe-answer rate moves. The pretrained model alone has 0.299 (0.266 to 0.332): the reported switch was its prompt-following, which supervised fine-tuning on one contract removes. The decoding check finds the as-trained models running past their turn, inflating refusal rates in both arms; first-turn scoring leaves the gaps unchanged. The scorer check finds that the phrase heuristic counts an echoed contract as refusal: echo is half of the pretrained model's 0.299, and an LLM judge finds its replies no more likely to refuse or deflect under either contract. A weight check finds the released PPO adapter untrained, so its rows re-measure the SFT and base models. The checks form a protocol for evaluating stacked-adapter models.
