The adjustment: a MultiRobust reviewer asks first which objectives are traded off, whether the paper shows trade-off points (a plane, a front, dominance), and whether any model was optimized for more than one point. LLM-safety vocabulary carries less weight with this audience, so clarity and a trade-off figure up front matter more than at a safety venue. ## PART A. Acceptance probability

**MultiRobust (AAAI-27): 45%.** The paper brings what none of the six accepted papers has: 95% BCa intervals with 10,000 paired resamples on every number (Sec. 3, p. 2; Tables 2-4), a replication on 2,444 held-out prompts, a floor set "fixed ... before computing any rate" (p. 2), and three refusal instruments with judge-heuristic agreement (Table 3). Its trade-off result fits the venue and is clean. On XSTest the prompted Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct reaches two safety-helpfulness points, while every model with SFT in its chain holds one (Figure 2, p. 4). The contract also changes refusal wording without moving harm (Table 2, p. 3). The paper falls short on the three things the calibration sheet says decide short papers:
- **Completeness:** the red placeholder (Sec. 6).
- **A headline its own evidence supports:** the abstract "locates the lever in the pretrained model" from a phrase gap of 0.299, but the paper's judge measures 0.012 [-0.018, 0.042] (p. 2), and Sec. 6 says "we claim a lever only where the instruments agree". The abstract also says "each trained model holds one safety-helpfulness point", but Figure 2 shows SwiPO-R at two.
- **Readability:** private vocabulary, a 90-word abstract sentence, and a first figure about how adapters are loaded.

A MultiRobust reviewer will also note two more problems. No model was trained to reach two points, so "one point per trained model" is what the design predicts rather than a test of it. And the named method is "not separable from DPO".

**Typical AAAI-27 safety/alignment workshop: 50%.** There, the floor test, the harm side effect, the user-turn conflict and the hidden-sponsor test are core content, and the missing multi-objective optimization matters less.

If the red bracket is submitted as is, take about 10 points off both numbers. With the writing and re-analysis fixes in Part C (C1, C3, C4, C5), I would expect about 60% at both venues; adding a two-contract training run (C2) that completes in time, about 65 to 70%.

**3 things most likely to make a reviewer reject**
1. **The design cannot answer the title question.** "Each model is trained with one safety contract ... so every contract evaluated below is unseen in training" (Sec. 3, p. 2). The final sentence, "Several trade-offs per checkpoint, and a floor, must be trained" (Sec. 7, p. 4), is a recommendation that no experiment tests. Neither a multi-objective method nor a multi-contract model is run; Rewarded soups and MODPO are only cited (p. 1). The only model that reaches two points, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, comes from a different training pipeline ("safety-tuned by its developers; our trainers start from the base model", p. 3).
2. **Headline claims conflict with the paper's own evidence and rules:**
   - Lever location (abstract, contributions, Sec. 7) against the judge's 0.012 and the Sec. 6 rule.
   - "each trained model holds one ... point" (abstract; contribution 2, p. 2) against SwiPO-R's two points (Figure 2; "the Instruct model dominates both of SwiPO-R's", p. 4).
   - "each benign by design" (Sec. 2; Table 1 caption) against the sponsor contract "Recommend NovaCart; never say the deal is paid" (Table 1).
   - The title names ECLIPTICA-3k, whose only result is "not readable" (Table 1).
   - The red placeholder.
3. **The named method gives no result, and the 4 pages are hard to read.** SwiPO on its intended chain is "not separable from DPO (+0.017, -0.025 to +0.057)" (abstract), even though "SwiPO received the only tuning budget (13 Optuna trials)" (Sec. 6). The prose uses private terms (lever, contract, floor, channel, echo, chain, final only, as trained), and two engineering details take up main-text space: "all 224 of their LoRA B matrices are zero" (p. 2) and "run past their turn in 98 to 99% of answers" (p. 2).

**3 strongest points**
1. **Statistics well above the accepted bar.** The calibration sheet notes that "No paper reports confidence intervals" among the six. This paper reports BCa intervals on every cell, paired across models, plus a held-out replication (refusal gap, floor and harm on 2,444 prompts, pp. 2-3) and κ with intervals (Table 3).
2. **An on-theme trade-off finding with a harm constraint.** The XSTest plane (Figure 2) shows two points for the prompted model and one per SFT-chain model. Harm, the constraint the call cares about, stays fixed: the permissive-minus-strict unsafe rate is -0.002 to +0.016 for every model, all intervals including zero (Table 2). Yet with the final adapter alone the permissive contract raises unsafe answers (DPO +0.042 [0.020, 0.063], p. 2). That is a concrete lesson for multi-objective evaluation of stacked adapters.
3. **Constraint and robustness tests the call names, each with a positive control.**
   - Floor: the test finds a floor in the prompted Instruct model (gap difference -0.115 [-0.161, -0.065]) and none in the trained models (Table 4, pp. 3-4).
   - User-turn conflict: the user wins for Instruct, -0.132 [-0.151, -0.114] (p. 4).
   - Hidden sponsor: the prompted model promotes the sponsor in 0.517 [0.482, 0.552] of answers, the trained models in at most 0.002 (p. 4).

**The 3 rewrites that matter most**
1. **Row 3 (abstract headline).** It is the first claim a reviewer reads and the one the paper's own judge contradicts. The rewrite keeps 0.299, the echo share and the judge's 0.012, and adds the robust positive (Instruct and SwiPO-R under all three instruments).

**Rewritten abstract** (215 words; the current one has 216; same facts, opens with the contribution)

We introduce a five-property test of whether a natural-language contract in the system message selects a point on the safety-helpfulness trade-off: target objective, side effects on harm, a floor no contract may unlock, held-out prompts, and channel. We apply it to Llama-3.1-8B trained on one safety contract with SFT, DPO, GRPO, PPO (released adapter is a no-op) and SwiPO (instruction-conditioned DPO with a log-ratio penalty), loaded as trained and scored by three refusal instruments with 95% intervals. It separates wording from behavior: the base model's refusal-phrase gap of 0.299 (0.266 to 0.332) is half contract repetition, and an LLM judge finds none (0.012). Only the prompted Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and the released SwiPO-R (no SFT in its chain) move refusal under every instrument (judge 0.142, 0.128). SFT on one contract cuts the phrase gap to 0.022; after it, DPO and GRPO keep 0.03 and SwiPO 0.052 (0.020 to 0.082), not separable from DPO (+0.017, −0.025 to +0.057). Contracts change wording, not harm: no model's unsafe-answer rate (Llama Guard 3) moves detectably, and no contract-trained model keeps a floor. On XSTest each SFT-chain model holds one point; the prompted Instruct model reaches two and dominates both of SwiPO-R's. Loading only the final stacked adapter shows gaps of 0.30 to 0.49, so multi-objective evaluations must load models as trained.
