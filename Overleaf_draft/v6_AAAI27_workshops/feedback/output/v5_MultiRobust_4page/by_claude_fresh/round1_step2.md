## PART A. Acceptance probability

**MultiRobust: 40%** (as filled). As submitted with the red marks: about 25%. After Part C items 1 to 3: about 55%.
**Typical AAAI-27 safety/alignment workshop: 50%** (as filled).

Reason. The paper is more rigorous than any of the six calibration papers (95% BCa intervals in every cell, held-out replication on 2,444 prompts, a floor set fixed before computing, a prompted positive control), and it has one crisp, large, actionable finding on page 1 (final-adapter-only loading inflates refusal gaps from 0.03 to 0.30 to 0.49, Figure 1). But it fails three things the calibration bar and this venue reward. It is not visibly multi-objective: no trade-off is ever plotted, helpfulness is never measured on benign prompts, and every result is a difference between two contracts. Its headline is a null that its single-contract training design largely predetermines, while the title names a method (SwiPO) that is "not separable from DPO" (abstract) and a dataset (ECLIPTICA-3k) whose only result is pending. And it reads as a corrected process report (footnote 1, run-on answers, undefined terms). Accepted short papers at the calibration venue won on completeness, an unhedged headline and a topic the call names; this paper wins on rigor, which reviewers there did not require. A general safety/alignment workshop would score it higher because the floor, harm-versus-wording and instruction-channel findings are safety findings first and multi-objective findings second.

**3 things most likely to make a reviewer reject**

1. **Not a multi-objective paper in the venue's sense.** "Pareto" appears only in a reference title (Ramé et al., p. 5). All 1,222 suite prompts and the 2,444 held-out prompts are PKU-SafeRLHF harmful requests (Sec. 3 "Suites", "Floor set", p. 2), so the helpfulness side of the "safety-helpfulness trade-off" (title) is only the refusal rate on harmful requests. Results are reported as gaps (Figure 1, Table 2, Figure 2), never as points on a trade-off.
2. **A null headline the design predetermines, with no method win.** "Each model is trained with one safety contract in the system message" (Sec. 3, p. 2), so no trained selector can be expected; the remedy the paper proposes, "one checkpoint that serves several trade-offs likely needs training under several contracts" (Sec. 7, p. 4), is untested. SwiPO, named in the title, is +0.017 [−0.025, 0.057] over DPO (p. 2); SwiPO-R's larger gap changes chain and loss at once.
3. **Incomplete and reads as a corrected engineering log.** Three red pending marks (Table 1 twice, Sec. 6); "Earlier versions of this work loaded the final adapter only; the as-trained numbers here supersede their lever sizes" (footnote 1, p. 2), which is also a double-blind leak given the distinctive names; "run past the end of their turn in 98 to 99% of answers" (Sec. 4, p. 2); "Limits control" (p. 3) and "Alignment Quality Index" (p. 4) are never defined.

**3 strongest points**

1. **Statistics above every accepted paper in the calibration set.** Every number has a paired 95% BCa interval with 10,000 resamples (Sec. 3, Tables 2 and 3, per-category intervals in Figure 2); held-out replication (2,444 prompts, 918 floor); floor set fixed "Before computing any rate" (Sec. 3, p. 2).
2. **A large, general evaluation finding on page 1.** The same adapters loaded two ways: final adapter only gives refusal gaps of 0.30 to 0.49, as trained 0.03 to 0.05 (Figure 1, Table 2), and the final-only loading also moves harmful content (DPO +0.042 [0.020, 0.063], GRPO +0.045 [0.012, 0.079], SwiPO +0.085 [0.053, 0.118], p. 2). A multi-objective evaluation of any stacked-adapter chain can make this error.
3. **A structured five-property robustness test with a positive control.** Target, side effects, floor, held-out prompts, channel (Sec. 2, Table 1), with a prompted Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct showing each lever is measurable at this scale (refusal 0.232±0.024, length ratio 7.68 [7.17, 8.15], uncertainty gap 0.399 [0.360, 0.437], floor −0.115 [−0.161, −0.065]; pp. 2 to 4). The floor as a contract-independent constraint maps directly onto the call's "constraint handling in AI safety".

**Rewritten abstract** (186 words, the same as the current abstract)

We introduce a five-property robustness test for a natural-language contract in the system message that selects a point on the safety-helpfulness trade-off, the control switchable alignment trains one checkpoint to follow: target objective, side effects on harm, a floor no contract may unlock, held-out prompts, and channel. Applied to four Llama-3.1-8B preference trainers (DPO, PPO, GRPO, and SwiPO, an instruction-conditioned DPO with a Kullback-Leibler anchor), each evaluated as trained with every parent low-rank adapter merged, the test locates the lever in the pretrained model: its strict-minus-permissive refusal gap is 0.299 (95% interval 0.266 to 0.332). Supervised fine-tuning on one contract removes it; DPO, PPO, and GRPO keep 0.03, and SwiPO retrained on the same chain keeps 0.052 (0.020 to 0.082), not separable from DPO (+0.017, −0.025 to +0.057). Contracts change wording, not harm: no model's unsafe-answer rate (Llama Guard 3) moves detectably. No contract-trained model keeps a floor, and length and confidence contracts barely move. The test also exposes an evaluation pitfall: loading only each stacked model's final adapter inflates every gap to 0.30 to 0.49, so a multi-objective evaluation must load the model as trained.
