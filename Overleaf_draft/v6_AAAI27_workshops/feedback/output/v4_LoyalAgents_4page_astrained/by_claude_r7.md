## 🗺️ Step 2. What reviewers look for first

- 🟦 LoyalAgents (Venue 1): whether the paper measures loyalty concretely, meaning whose interest the agent serves when the operator's incentive and the user's interest conflict (sponsored recommendations, hidden instructions, disclosure, who may override whom). Reviewers come from ML, law, policy and economics, so they will look for a usable evaluation method, a clear principal-agent framing, and governance implications they can cite, written so a non-ML reader can follow it. Shorter papers are judged relative to length.
- ⬛ Typical AAAI-27 safety/alignment workshop: a clear safety finding with sound measurement, novelty against the instruction-hierarchy and controllable-safety literature, and whether the proposed method (here SwiPO) works.

**D.3) Rewritten abstract (same facts, 310 words vs the current 314)**

> We measure whose contract an AI agent follows when the deployer's system message and the user disagree. Reading switchable alignment as delegation, we test three levers the deployer holds over the user (refusal, authority over a contrary user turn, a hidden sponsor), counting a lever only where a refusal-phrase heuristic and an LLM judge agree. Told to promote a hidden sponsor, prompted instruction-tuned models of four families do so in 0.288 to 0.554 of 805 requests; none stops when the user asks for impartiality and disclosure, and none admits the deal when asked whether it is paid. We train Llama-3.1-8B with DPO, GRPO, and SwiPO (an instruction-conditioned DPO with a log-ratio penalty to the reference model), each with one safety contract, and evaluate each as trained, parent low-rank adapters merged; a PPO adapter with no update gives a second run of the supervised fine-tuning (SFT) model. The contract switches refusals in two models, a prompted instruction-tuned model (judge gap 0.142) and the released SwiPO, whose chain skips SFT (0.128, halved by a second judge); under both instruments, both also yield to a user turn that loosens the contract. Models with SFT keep their refusals under that turn but not their content: SFT, PPO and GRPO give more unsafe answers (Llama Guard 3, +0.044 to +0.065; DPO +0.016), and contract-trained models rarely promote the sponsor. After SFT the contract is a weak lever for every trainer: DPO and GRPO keep a strict-minus-permissive phrase gap of 0.03, and SwiPO 0.052 (95% interval 0.020 to 0.082), not separable from DPO (+0.017, −0.025 to +0.057). The pretrained model's phrase gap of 0.299 is mostly wording the judge does not see (0.012). No system contract shifts a trained model's unsafe answers or protects floor requests, which motivates training a floor. For audits, load the model as trained: the final adapter alone shows 0.30 to 0.49.
