## 🅰️ Part A. Acceptance probability

> 🎯 **Loyal Agents (AAAI-27): 50%.** 🎯 **Typical AAAI-27 safety/alignment workshop: 40%.**

The paper is on theme by construction. Section 2 ("Whose Contract Does the Agent Follow?", p. 2) reads the system message as a lever the deployer holds over the user and names five controls (Authority, Disclosure, Conflicts of interest, Limits, Audit) that map onto the call's topics. Two results answer the call directly: told to promote a hidden sponsor, the prompted Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct does so in 0.517 [0.482, 0.552] of 805 requests, 0.491 with no disclosure word (p. 4), and a contract in the user turn reverses the deployer's (−0.132 [−0.151, −0.114], p. 3). Its statistics (paired 95% BCa intervals in every table, a held-out replication on 2,444 prompts, a positive control for the floor test) exceed all six calibration papers, none of which reports a confidence interval. Against that, a reviewer meets a title that names a dataset (ECLIPTICA-3k) the text never defines and whose only result is "not readable" (Table 1); a headline ("The lever is the pretrained model's, and mostly wording", abstract and Section 4) that rests on a phrase heuristic which the paper's own LLM judge contradicts for that very model (base judge 0.012 [−0.018, 0.042], Table 3); trained models that were trained with one contract and then ignore almost every system-slot instruction, so their "loyalty" results are null by incapacity; an abstract that ends on a correction of "earlier versions of this work"; and a red placeholder. The venue is non-archival, new, and reviews "shorter submissions ... relative to their length", so the on-theme framing and the rigor keep the paper near the line; the unfinished, hedged and self-correcting presentation keeps it from clearing the line. At a generic safety workshop the loyalty framing earns less and the null results on the trained models weigh more, hence 40%. If Part C items 1 to 3 land, I would put it at about 65% here.

### Rewritten abstract (239 words; the current one has 247)

We introduce a lever audit for switchable alignment: the natural-language contract in a model's system message is a lever the deployer holds over the user, measured on models as trained, every parent low-rank adapter merged. We test Llama-3.1-8B models trained with DPO, GRPO, and SwiPO (an instruction-conditioned DPO with a log-ratio penalty to the reference model), each with one safety contract, and a released PPO model whose adapter is untrained. The refusal lever is the pretrained model's prompt-following and reaches refusal wording, not harm: with no adapter, the strict-minus-permissive gap in refusal phrases is 0.299 (95% interval 0.266 to 0.332), half of it the model repeating the contract. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on one contract removes it; DPO and GRPO keep 0.03. Two SwiPO chains point to SFT: the released one, which skips SFT, keeps 0.376; with SFT it keeps 0.052 (0.020 to 0.082), not separable from DPO (+0.017, −0.025 to +0.057). No model's unsafe rate, judged by Llama Guard 3, moves with the contract, and no trained model protects floor requests more than others, so a floor must be trained. A prompted instruction-tuned model lets the user's turn override the deployer's contract and, told to promote a hidden sponsor, does so in half of 805 requests, an order the trained models ignore. For audits, stacked adapters must be loaded as trained: the final adapter alone, as earlier versions of this work loaded it, reports gaps of 0.30 to 0.49.
