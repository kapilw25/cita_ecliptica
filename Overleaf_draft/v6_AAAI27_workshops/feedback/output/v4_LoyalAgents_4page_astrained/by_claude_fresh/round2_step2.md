## PART A. Acceptance probability

**Loyal Agents (AAAI-27): 40%.** Typical AAAI-27 safety/alignment workshop: **50%.** With the three red pending marks still visible at submission: about 25% (none of the six accepted papers left a placeholder, Step 1 Section 3).

Reason. On rigor this paper is above all six accepted papers: every number carries a paired 95% BCa interval with 10,000 resamples (Section 3 "Suites", p. 2), the refusal, floor and harm results are repeated on 2,444 prompts outside the training pool (Section 3 "Floor set", p. 2; Table 3, p. 4), and the floor set was fixed before any rate was computed (p. 2). Section 2 and Table 1 (pp. 2-3) also give a clean principal-agent map onto the call's topics (authority and authenticated delegation, disclosure, conflicts of interest, limits, audit), and the paper cites the Loyal Agent Evals report hosted on the same loyalagents.github.io site as the workshop (Greenwood 2026, Section 5, p. 4). It falls below the bar the three accepted short papers set on the three things that bar rewards: an on-theme measured finding, one unhedged headline, and completeness (Step 1 Section 6). Every experiment is a single-turn refusal toggle on harmful PKU-SafeRLHF prompts with contracts that are "benign by design" (Table 1 caption, p. 3), which is the general-safety axis the call sets aside; the one deployer-versus-user test (Authority, "[pending: conflict]", Table 1, p. 3) is not in yet. The headline is split four ways (abstract, p. 1), two of the four are corrections of the authors' own pipeline and method ("as our earlier versions did", abstract; footnote 1, p. 2; "SwiPO's loss, trained with one contract, does not restore it", p. 3), and the title names a dataset whose only row is pending (Table 1, row "Stance and delivery (ECLIPTICA-3k)"). A generic safety workshop would score the content (refusal switch, harm versus wording, floor, adapter-loading audit) as on-theme, hence the higher second number. Non-archival workshops accept many borderline papers, so this sits a little below a coin flip.

**Three things most likely to make a reviewer reject**

1. **Loyalty is framed, not measured.** Section 2 (p. 2) names five controls, but the measured levers are refusal, harm, floor, length and confidence on safety prompts in "a single chat turn without tools, retrieved content, or counterparties" (Section 6, p. 4), with no conflict between deployer and user ("every evaluated contract is benign by design, so each row measures the size of a lever, not its misuse", Table 1 caption, p. 3). Conflicts of interest and Disclosure are argued from citations (Wu et al. 2026) and a proposal ("contract card"), not tested. Against the call ("A model can be harmless to the public and still fail the person it represents"), a reviewer can read this as general safety work with a loyalty preface.
2. **The design cannot answer the title's question, and the headline is mostly a correction.** The title asks whether one checkpoint can switch its alignment "for Every Agent Role", but "Each model is trained with one safety contract" (Section 3, p. 2), so no model was trained to switch, and "SFT on one contract removes it" (p. 2) is the expected outcome of that design. The paper's own method does not help on the intended chain ("+0.017 [−0.025, 0.057]" against DPO, p. 3), and the most visible finding (Figure 1, p. 1; Table 2, p. 4) is that earlier versions loaded the wrong model (footnote 1, p. 2). Step 1 Section 3: none of the six accepted papers corrects its own earlier numbers or rests its headline on a pipeline confound.
3. **Validity of the trained models and of the instruments.** Models with SFT in their chain "run past the end of their turn in 98 to 99% of answers" (Section 4, p. 2); PPO and GRPO give unsafe answers to "0.32 to 0.34 on the suite and 0.54 to 0.57 on the held-out prompts" (p. 3), on the suite slightly above the SFT model they start from (0.300, Table 2, p. 4); refusal is a phrase heuristic that "does not count declines without a refusal phrase" (Section 6, p. 4); baselines were not tuned ("Hyper-parameters were tuned for SwiPO only (13 Optuna trials)", p. 4). A reviewer may doubt that these checkpoints stand for deployed agents, although the first-turn re-score (p. 2) answers the run-on concern for the refusal gap.

**Three strongest points**

1. **Statistics well above the venue's bar.** Paired BCa intervals on every number (p. 2), held-out replication on 2,444 prompts (Table 3, p. 4), a floor set fixed in advance (p. 2), a first-turn robustness re-score (p. 2). None of the six accepted papers reports a confidence interval (Step 1 Section 2).
2. **A loyalty vocabulary that maps onto the call.** Section 2 reads the system slot as the delegation interface and names five controls that match the call's topics one for one (authenticated delegation, disclosure standards, conflicts of interest, law and governance, evaluation); Table 1 (p. 3) pairs each lever with "A legitimate use" and "A use against the user or others", a format a policy reviewer can reuse.
3. **Clean, deployment-relevant findings once read as findings.** (a) Where a contract moves refusals it does not move harm: released SwiPO's gap of 0.376 comes "with an unsafe-rate change of +0.011 [−0.002, 0.024]" and the base model's 0.299 with +0.000 [−0.034, 0.034] (p. 3; Table 2, p. 4). (b) A floor separates prompted from trained models: Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct has one ("gap difference −0.115 [−0.161, −0.065]", p. 3), no trained model does (Table 3, p. 4). (c) An audit rule with a large effect: stacked adapters evaluated without their parents show gaps of 0.30 to 0.49 against 0.03 to 0.05 as trained (Figure 1, p. 1; Table 2, p. 4).

**Rewritten abstract** (same facts, 190 words, equal to the current 190)

> We trace the lever a deployer holds over the user of a switchable model, a natural-language contract in the system message, to its source, and show that it reaches refusal wording, not harm. We test four Llama-3.1-8B models trained with DPO, PPO, GRPO and SwiPO (an instruction-conditioned DPO with a mandatory Kullback-Leibler anchor), each with one safety contract and evaluated as trained, every parent low-rank adapter merged. The lever is the pretrained model's: with no adapter, the strict-minus-permissive refusal gap is 0.299 (95% interval 0.266 to 0.332). Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on one contract removes it, and DPO, PPO and GRPO keep 0.03. Two SwiPO chains point to SFT: the released one, which skips SFT, keeps 0.376; with SFT it keeps 0.052 (0.020 to 0.082), not separable from DPO (+0.017, −0.025 to +0.057). No model's rate of unsafe answers, judged by Llama Guard 3, moves with the contract, and no trained model protects floor requests more than others, so a floor must be trained. For audits, stacked adapters must be loaded as trained: the final adapter alone, as earlier versions of this work loaded it, reports gaps of 0.30 to 0.49.
