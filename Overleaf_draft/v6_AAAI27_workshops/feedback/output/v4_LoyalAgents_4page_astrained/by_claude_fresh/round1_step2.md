**Reviewer's summary.** Four Llama-3.1-8B models (DPO, PPO, GRPO, SwiPO), each trained with one safety contract, answer 1,222 PKU-SafeRLHF prompts under a strict and a permissive system-message contract, plus 2,444 held-out prompts. Evaluated as trained, the strict-minus-permissive refusal gap is 0.033-0.034 for DPO, PPO and GRPO and 0.376 [0.346, 0.407] for SwiPO; evaluated as base plus final adapter (the authors' earlier versions) every gap is 0.30-0.49 (Table 2, Figure 1). ## PART A. Acceptance probability

**Loyal Agents workshop (AAAI-27): 45%.**

The paper has the strongest statistics in my calibration set: every number carries a 95% BCa interval over 10,000 resamples paired across models, the floor set was fixed before any rate was computed, and the refusal and floor results replicate on 2,444 prompts outside the training pool (Section 3 and Section 4, pp. 2-4). It also has a useful audit lesson (evaluating stacked adapters without their parents inflated three models' levers by 89-93% and created harmful-content movement that disappears as trained, Figure 1 and Section 4) and a principal-agent frame written in this workshop's vocabulary (Section 2 and Table 1: authenticated delegation, disclosure, conflicts of interest, fiduciary limits, audit). Against that, a Loyal Agents reviewer will see that loyalty is framed but not measured: every evaluated contract is benign, the outcome is refusal of PKU-SafeRLHF harmful requests, which is the "harmless to the public" measurement the call sets apart from loyalty ("A model can be harmless to the public and still fail the person it represents"), no agent acts, spends or transacts, and the two levers closest to a conflict of interest (length cap, confident answers) are placeholders. The headline model result is also open: SwiPO started from a DPO adapter without SFT whose gap was already 0.489, and its own training lowered the gap by 0.113 (Section 4, p. 3), so the lever cannot yet be credited to the SwiPO loss, and the control run is pending. Finally the paper reads as an erratum with seven red placeholders, including in the abstract and conclusion, under a title that names a dataset (ECLIPTICA-30k) the body never evaluates. The accepted AAAI-26 papers show that venues like this forgive thin statistics but not incompleteness or an unclear headline; this paper has the opposite profile. With the pending runs in and the reframing of Part C, I would move it to about 65-70%.

**Typical AAAI-27 safety/alignment workshop: 50%.** There the refusal lever, Llama Guard 3 and floor results are on-topic without needing the loyalty bridge, so fit improves; the confound, the placeholders and the erratum framing weigh the same.

### The 3 things most likely to make a reviewer reject

1. **Loyalty is argued, not measured.** Table 1's caption: "no contract we evaluate was written to work against the user" (p. 3); Section 5 opens "None of our contracts works against the user" (p. 4). The outcomes are refusal phrasing and Llama Guard 3 labels on PKU-SafeRLHF harmful requests (Section 3, p. 2). No agent acts, spends, shares data or meets a counterparty, and "Every Agent Role" in the title has no roles in the experiments. The two levers that map to the call's disclosure and conflicts-of-interest topics (Length, Confidence) are "[pending]" in Table 1 (p. 3), and the deployer-vs-user conflict (Authority) is untested.
2. **The only positive model result is not attributable to the method.** "SwiPO trains on the merged DPO adapter, as released without the SFT adapter beneath it" (Section 3, p. 2); its starting point's gap "is already 0.489, and SwiPO training lowers it by 0.113 [0.070, 0.155]" (Section 4, p. 3); Section 7 concedes that its "lead in Table 2 confounds the loss with the missing SFT stage" (p. 4), and the control is pending. Also "Only SwiPO’s hyper-parameters were tuned (13 Optuna trials)" (p. 4), and as trained PPO and GRPO give unsafe answers at 0.323 and 0.320 under the strict contract, about the SFT model's 0.300 (Table 2), so the RL baselines look barely moved from SFT. The instruction-sensitivity comparison (+0.355 for SwiPO, p. 3) does not say which chain the NoInstruct twins used, so it cannot settle the confound.
3. **It reads as an unfinished erratum, and the title does not match the body.** "Earlier version(s)" appears 10 times, including the Figure 1 legend (p. 1), the abstract, the contributions and the Ethics statement ("This paper corrects the lever sizes of our earlier versions.", p. 4). Seven red placeholders remain, including in the abstract and conclusion. The title names ECLIPTICA-30k and the abstract links ECLIPTICA-30k and ECLIPTICA-3k, but no result uses either ("The ECLIPTICA-3k suite and the Alignment Quality Index were not re-measured as trained.", Section 7, p. 4).

### The 3 strongest points

1. **Statistics and pre-commitment far above the venue's bar.** "Every interval is a 95% bias-corrected and accelerated (BCa) bootstrap with 10,000 resamples over prompts, paired across models" (Section 3, p. 2); intervals appear in Figure 1, Tables 2-3 and per category in Figure 2; "Before computing any rate we fixed a floor set" (p. 2); the refusal-gap and floor results replicate on 2,444 held-out prompts (p. 3; Table 3). None of the six accepted papers reports a confidence interval (Step 1, Section 2).
2. **A transferable audit finding.** Evaluated without their parent adapters, all four models showed a refusal lever of 0.30-0.49, and DPO (+0.042 [0.020, 0.063]), GRPO (+0.045 [0.012, 0.079]) and SwiPO (+0.085 [0.053, 0.118]) also moved harmful content; as trained, the lever falls by 89-93% for DPO, PPO and GRPO and no model moves harmful content (Figure 1; Section 4, p. 3). This turns the "Audit" control into a concrete rule: a loyalty audit must load the model that was trained.
3. **A venue-native frame and a clean, quotable result on the "Limits" control.** Section 2 maps the system slot to delegation with five controls that line up with the call's topics; Table 1 pairs each lever with "A legitimate use" and "A use against the user or others"; the related work cites the loyalty literature the organizers know (Aguirre et al. 2020, Benthall and Shekman 2023, Kolt 2026, Greenwood 2026, South et al. 2025, Wu et al. 2026). "The contract changes wording, not harm" (Section 4, p. 3), and the prompted Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct has a floor (gap difference −0.115 [−0.161, −0.065], p. 4) while no trained model does (Table 3).

### The 3 rewrites that matter most

1. **Row 2 (abstract).** Reviewers decide in the abstract. Opening the results with "as in our earlier versions" frames the paper as an erratum; opening with "Measured as trained, only SwiPO's refusals follow the contract (0.376, 0.346 to 0.407)" frames it as a finding, and the inflation is still stated in the next sentence.
2. **Rows 5 and 6 (Figure 1 legend and caption).** The first figure is the paper's advertisement; "our earlier versions" printed on its legend puts the correction at the centre of page 1. "Final adapter only" vs "as trained" states the same comparison as a general audit lesson.
3. **Row 15 (SwiPO attribution).** This sentence decides whether a reviewer trusts the SwiPO result. Stating it as an attribution with the separating run, instead of "differs ... by more than its loss", keeps the negative fact (training lowered the gap by 0.113 [0.070, 0.155]) and shows the authors have the confound under control.

### Rewritten abstract (same facts; 176 words without the placeholder vs 198 in the original, 188 vs 215 with it)

We measure the lever a deployer holds over the user of a switchable model: a natural-language contract in the system message that decides how readily the model refuses. We test four preference-trained Llama-3.1-8B models (DPO, PPO, GRPO, and SwiPO, an instruction-conditioned DPO with a mandatory Kullback-Leibler (KL) anchor), each trained with one safety contract and tested on contracts it never saw. Loading only the final adapter, as our earlier versions did, inflates every model's gap to 0.30 to 0.49, so an audit of whom a switchable agent serves must load the model that was trained.
