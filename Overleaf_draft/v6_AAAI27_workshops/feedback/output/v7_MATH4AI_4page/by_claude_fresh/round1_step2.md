**Verdict on the math.** Correct up to fixable slips: the minimizers in Prop. 1 and Cor. 1 do not exist as written, and the Eq. 2 term is described as a divergence anchor when it is a signed off-policy log-ratio that pushes likelihood down on both answers. Trivial in content: Prop. 1 is linearity of expectation plus decoupled minimization over a product set; Cor. 1 says a loss that never evaluates a conditional does not constrain it. At a mathematical-foundations venue these are remarks, not results. The question this venue cares about, how far an unseen contract's switch moves through shared parameters as a function of contract similarity and parameter displacement, is left as "up to drift through shared parameters" (p. 2). That clause makes the prediction impossible to falsify: +0.017 and -0.113 are both called "as predicted" (p. 2), and SFT's fall from 0.299 to 0.022 (Table 2) is the same separable-loss setting with 93% drift.

### A.2 The 3 things most likely to make a reviewer reject

1. **The mathematics is a remark, and the shared-parameter case is not analyzed** (p. 2, Sec. 2). Prop. 1 and Cor. 1 are one-line facts. The regime that applies to every trained model in the paper is covered only by "up to drift through shared parameters", which gives no bound, so no outcome could falsify the prediction (A.0 row 6). For a venue that asks math to "explain and shape" AI, this is the central weakness.
2. **Formal errors a math reviewer will catch** (p. 2, Eq. 2 and Cor. 1). The "mandatory anchor ... a proxy for the divergence" is a signed log-ratio on data samples, whose minimization lowers the likelihood of both answers and makes L unbounded below (A.0 row 3). So the "minimizer" language of Prop. 1 and Cor. 1 has no object, and "is a minimizer, so training leaves it at its initialization" is a non sequitur (A.0 rows 2 and 5). One such slip in a 0.4-column math section undermines trust in the rest.
3. **The tested prediction rests on the instrument the paper itself distrusts, and one number points the other way** (Sec. 2 p. 2; Table 3 p. 3; Sec. 7 p. 4). The corollary test in Sec. 2 uses only the phrase heuristic, which is half contract echo for the base model and agrees with the LLM judge at κ 0.03 to 0.22 for every trained model (Table 3). Under the judge, the base model has no gap (0.012 [-0.018, 0.042]) but SwiPO-R has 0.128 [0.095, 0.162]. The judge gap of SwiPO-R's starting point (DPO adapter alone, phrase 0.489) is not reported. If that gap is near zero, SwiPO-R gained a switch under the judge, contradicting "Neither chain gains a switch" (abstract, p. 2) and "the only sizable switch we find is the pretrained model's prompt-following" (Sec. 7). Add to this that the title dataset, ECLIPTICA-3k, appears only in a pending row (Table 1) and the human check is pending (Sec. 6).

### A.3 The 3 strongest points

1. **A theory-then-test design with a clean control** (p. 2, Sec. 3): "We train SwiPO on two chains that differ only in the SFT adapter, with the same data and hyper-parameters ... The pair separates the effect of the chain from the effect of the loss." The prediction is checked on SwiPO-R, SwiPO-S, DPO, PPO and GRPO as trained (Table 2, Figure 1), and the direction holds on both SwiPO chains (+0.017 [-0.025, 0.057]; -0.113 [-0.155, -0.070], p. 2).
2. **Statistics no calibration paper approaches** (Sec. 3, Tables 2-4): paired 95% BCa intervals with 10,000 resamples on every number; a floor set "fixed before computing any rate" (p. 2); held-out replication on 2,444 unseen prompts (pp. 2-4); three refusal instruments with κ (Table 3); harm measured separately by Llama Guard 3 (Table 2). The echo finding (41% of base answers repeat the contract, Table 3) is a useful methodological warning for anyone who measures steerability with refusal phrases.
3. **A crisp, actionable message for agents acting for principals** (Sec. 1, Sec. 7): one-contract training cannot be expected to create a switch, so "a switchable checkpoint needs training tuples that pair one request with several contracts and different preferred answers" (p. 4). It fits the call's "mathematical foundations of AI agents" and "stability and reliability of agentic AI" topics once the math carries more weight.

**C1. Turn "up to drift through shared parameters" into a statement with a measurable term.**
- What it shows: that the corollary predicts the SIZE and SIGN of each stage's change, not only "near". Statement (Proposition 2): let g(θ) = E_X[s_θ(I_s, X) - s_θ(I_p, X)] be a smooth surrogate of the gap, with s the log-odds of a fixed refusal prefix versus a fixed compliance prefix under contract I. Under gradient flow on L_{I0}, dg/dt = -⟨∇g(θ_t), ∇L_{I0}(θ_t)⟩, so the gap can change only through the alignment of its gradient with the training-contract gradient; to first order over a run, Δg ≈ ⟨∇g(θ_0), Δθ⟩ and |Δg| ≤ ‖∇g(θ_0)‖ ‖Δθ‖ plus a second-order term. Cor. 1 is the special case ∇g ⊥ ∇L (independent conditionals). The proof is three lines (chain rule, Cauchy-Schwarz, Taylor), still elementary, but it gives the venue what it asks for: a quantity that predicts trained models and can fail.
- What it needs: the existing checkpoints of all six stages (SFT, DPO, PPO, GRPO, SwiPO-R, SwiPO-S), each with its starting point θ_0 and merged LoRA delta Δθ. > We prove that a contract-conditioned preference loss separates by contract: for a policy class with independent conditionals per contract, the loss splits per contract, so each contract's behavior is fitted only by its own data, and with a single training contract the loss places no constraint on behavior under any other contract. Switchable alignment asks exactly this of one checkpoint: conditioned on a natural-language contract I, it should change behavior when I changes. The corollary predicts that after one-contract training the switch stays near that of the starting model. We test this on four preference-trained Llama-3.1-8B models (DPO, PPO, GRPO, and SwiPO, an instruction-conditioned DPO with a log-ratio regularizer), each trained with one safety contract and evaluated as trained. Consistent with the corollary, SwiPO gains no switch on either chain: from a strict-minus-permissive refusal gap of 0.034 it ends at 0.052 (difference +0.017, 95% interval −0.025 to +0.057), and from 0.489 at 0.376 (−0.113, −0.155 to −0.070). The starting switches trace to the pretrained model, whose refusal-phrase gap of 0.299 (0.266 to 0.332) is half contract echo and is removed by supervised fine-tuning on one contract. A switchable checkpoint therefore needs training data with several contracts per request, the condition the separability result makes precise.
