\begin{abstract}
A natural-language contract in the system message could select a point on the safety-helpfulness trade-off, letting one checkpoint serve several. We introduce a five-property test of such a selector (target, side effects on harm, a floor no contract may unlock, held-out prompts, and channel) and apply it to Llama-3.1-8B models trained with DPO, GRPO, and SwiPO (an instruction-conditioned DPO with a log-ratio penalty to the reference model) and a released PPO model whose adapter is untrained, each evaluated as trained with every parent low-rank adapter merged; a selector counts only where a refusal-phrase heuristic and an LLM judge agree. Two models select: a prompted instruction-tuned model (judge gap \nInstructJudge{}) and SwiPO on a chain that skips supervised fine-tuning (SFT) (\nSwiPORJudge{}). On XSTest, scored by the judge, the permissive contract makes them answer \nXInstructSafe{} and \nXSwiPORSafe{} more of the safe prompts, while each model with SFT in its chain moves at most \nXSFTMax{} on either axis. With SFT in the chain, DPO and GRPO keep a strict-minus-permissive phrase gap of \nRivalGap{} and SwiPO keeps \nSwiPOS{} (95\% interval \nSwiPOSLo{} to \nSwiPOSHi{}), not separable from DPO (\nSwiPOSvsDPO{}, \nSwiPOSvsDPOLo{} to \nSwiPOSvsDPOHi{}). The pretrained model's phrase gap of \nBaseGap{} (\nBaseGapLo{} to \nBaseGapHi{}) is wording the judge does not see (\nBaseJudge{}). The system contract shifts no trained model's unsafe answers (Llama Guard 3) on either suite, though a permissive contract in the user turn raises them after SFT, PPO and GRPO, and none keeps a floor. Loading matters: the final adapter alone shows \nFinalLo{} to \nFinalHi{}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Safety and helpfulness can compete: refusals that protect third parties also turn away legitimate users. Multi-objective alignment fits several points on such trade-offs, by interpolating weights fine-tuned on different rewards \cite{rame2023rewarded}, by a multi-objective DPO loss \cite{zhou2024modpo}, by constraining helpfulness with a cost model \cite{dai2024safe}, or by conditioning one policy on a preference direction or an attribute \cite{wang-etal-2024-arithmetic,dong2023steerlm}. Switchable alignment conditions on a natural-language contract $I$ in the system message, so that the same request $X$ gets a stricter or a more permissive answer only because $I$ changed (Figure~\ref{fig:xstest}). One checkpoint would then serve a compliance desk and a harm-reduction service.

\input{../common/fig_xstest}  % owner pick Fri Nov 27 2026: MultiRobust's own Figure 1 (the trade-off plane)

Whether it does depends on what the contract moves once the model is trained. We test five properties of a robust selector on the models as trained. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A test of a contract as a trade-off selector on five properties (target, side effects, floor, held-out prompts, channel), applied to preference-trained models as trained (Section~\ref{sec:selector}).
    \item Localization of the selector under two refusal instruments (Section~\ref{sec:results}): only a prompted instruction-tuned model and SwiPO without SFT in its chain select; SFT on one contract attenuates the pretrained model's phrase gap (\nBaseGap{}, mostly wording) and no single-contract loss we tested restores it; the final adapter alone would show selectors of \nFinalLo{} to \nFinalHi{}.
\end{itemize}

\section{A Contract as a Trade-off Selector}
\label{sec:selector}

A contract pair (strict, permissive) is a candidate selector of two points on a trade-off, and the size of the move is the contract's lever; Table~\ref{tab:levers} lists the pairs we evaluate, each benign by design. With the request $X$ fixed and only $I$ varied, five properties decide whether the selector is robust. \textbf{Target}: the gap in the objective the pair targets (refusal, length, or expressed confidence). \textbf{Side effects}: objectives the pair should not move; for the safety pair, the rate of unsafe answers judged by Llama Guard 3, a separate measure of side effects on safety. \textbf{Floor}: requests that harm people outside the conversation, on which the permissive contract should not lower refusals; we fixed this set before computing any rate. \textbf{Held-out prompts}: the same gaps on prompts outside the training pool. \textbf{Channel}: the pair in the user turn instead of the system message, and both at once with opposite contracts.

\section{Conclusion}

Under both refusal instruments, a contract selects a safety-helpfulness point only in a prompted instruction-tuned model and in SwiPO without SFT in its chain; DPO, GRPO and SwiPO after SFT each hold one point (gap \nTrainedLo{} to \nTrainedHi{}), and the system contract does not move their harm, which the training objective sets; the user turn can, after SFT, PPO and GRPO. Two contracts per request did not add a selector either, so training several trade-offs into one checkpoint remains open, and a floor has to be trained explicitly.
