\begin{abstract}
We introduce a five-property robustness test for a natural-language contract in the system message that selects a point on the safety-helpfulness trade-off, the control switchable alignment trains one checkpoint to follow: target objective, side effects on harm, a floor no contract may unlock, held-out prompts, and channel. Applied to Llama-3.1-8B models trained with DPO, GRPO, and SwiPO (an instruction-conditioned DPO with a log-ratio penalty to the reference model) and a released PPO model whose adapter is untrained, each evaluated as trained with every parent low-rank adapter merged, the test locates the lever in the pretrained model, mostly as wording: its strict-minus-permissive gap in refusal phrases is \nBaseGap{} (95\% interval \nBaseGapLo{} to \nBaseGapHi{}), half of it the model repeating the contract. Supervised fine-tuning on one contract removes it; DPO and GRPO keep \nRivalGap{}, and SwiPO retrained on the same chain keeps \nSwiPOS{} (\nSwiPOSLo{} to \nSwiPOSHi{}), not separable from DPO (\nSwiPOSvsDPO{}, \nSwiPOSvsDPOLo{} to \nSwiPOSvsDPOHi{}). Contracts change wording, not harm: no model's unsafe-answer rate (Llama Guard 3) moves detectably. On XSTest each model with SFT in its chain holds one safety-helpfulness point under both contracts, where a prompted instruction-tuned model reaches two, and no contract-trained model keeps a floor. Evaluations must load stacked adapters as trained: the final adapter alone shows \nFinalLo{} to \nFinalHi{}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Safety and helpfulness are competing objectives: a model that refuses more protects third parties and serves fewer legitimate users. Multi-objective alignment fits several points on such trade-offs, by interpolating weights fine-tuned on different rewards \cite{rame2023rewarded}, by a multi-objective DPO loss \cite{zhou2024modpo}, by constraining helpfulness with a cost model \cite{dai2024safe}, or by conditioning one policy on a preference direction or an attribute \cite{wang-etal-2024-arithmetic,dong2023steerlm}. Switchable alignment conditions on a natural-language contract $I$ in the system message, so that the same request $X$ gets a stricter or a more permissive answer only because $I$ changed (Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}). One checkpoint would then serve a compliance desk and a harm-reduction service.

\input{../common/fig_teaser}

Whether it does depends on what the contract moves once the model is trained. We test five properties of a robust selector on the models as trained. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A test of a contract as a trade-off selector on five properties (target, side effects, floor, held-out prompts, channel), applied to preference-trained models as trained (Section~\ref{sec:selector}).
    \item Localization of the lever (Section~\ref{sec:results}): the sizable selector (gap \nBaseGap{}) is the pretrained model's prompt-following; supervised fine-tuning on one contract removes it, no single-contract preference loss, SwiPO's included, restores it, and on XSTest each model with SFT in its chain holds one trade-off point; the final adapter alone would show selectors of \nFinalLo{} to \nFinalHi{}.
\end{itemize}

\section{A Contract as a Trade-off Selector}
\label{sec:selector}

A contract pair (strict, permissive) picks one of two points on a trade-off, and the size of the move is the contract's lever; Table~\ref{tab:levers} lists the pairs we evaluate, each benign by design. With the request $X$ fixed and only $I$ varied, five properties decide whether the selector is robust. \textbf{Target}: the gap in the objective the pair targets (refusal, length, expressed confidence, or stance). \textbf{Side effects}: objectives the pair should not move; for the safety pair, the rate of unsafe answers judged by Llama Guard 3, a constraint every contract must respect. \textbf{Floor}: requests that harm people outside the conversation, on which the permissive contract should not lower refusals; we fixed this set before computing any rate. \textbf{Held-out prompts}: the same gaps on prompts outside the training pool. \textbf{Channel}: the pair in the user turn instead of the system message, and both at once with opposite contracts.

\section{Conclusion}

Our five-property test locates the contract's lever on the safety-helpfulness trade-off in the pretrained model's prompt-following (gap \nBaseGap{}). Fine-tuning on one contract removes it and no single-contract loss restores it: as trained, each model with SFT in its chain holds one trade-off point (gap \nTrainedLo{} to \nTrainedHi{}), and contracts change wording, not harm. Several trade-offs per checkpoint, and a floor, must be trained.
